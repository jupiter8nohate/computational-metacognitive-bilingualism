## PROLOGUE // THE ROOM THAT DID NOT EXIST

The first weapon made no sound.

Not because it was invisible. The weapon was quite visible. It had a welcome screen, a customer-service department, a tasteful accessibility statement, and a gold medal from a committee that had inspected its own gold medals.

It was called **THE WELLNESS MIRROR**.

It appeared above every hospital bed in the Ministry's new Department of Manufactured Reality. Below its polished glass, ten brass labels glittered under museum lighting.

Each label bore the name of a condition that did not exist.

And because the labels did not exist, the Ministry had been allowed to invent both the definition and the treatment. Bureaucracy is wonderful at this. Give an uncertainty a Latin-looking hat and somebody will eventually invoice the hat.

Mara arrived with a paper notebook. Inspector Edda Vale came carrying the authentication ledger. Nia brought a blue bird with a crooked wing. Jupiter Hudson brought the one document the Ministry hated most: a blank form containing the words **SHOW YOUR EVIDENCE**.

Behind the observation window, people waited to be heard.

Some were frightened. Some were exhausted. Some were experiencing symptoms that needed real medical attention. Others were simply angry that the Ministry had classified ordinary decisions as diseases.

The common feature was not illness.

The common feature was **a system trying to use the same answer for ten different human beings**.

The wall chimed.

~~~text
DEPARTMENT OF MANUFACTURED REALITY
AUDIT SESSION: 2084-10
CASE COUNT: 10
STATUS: PRECLASSIFIED
APPEAL RIGHTS: AUTOMATED
HUMAN REVIEW: OPTIONAL

WARNING: PERSONHOOD NOT FOUND IN MODEL OUTPUT
~~~

Jupiter looked at the last line.

"At least," he said, "one of us finally admits the model has a missing field."

## I // ALGOPHRENIA™: THE MEMORY THAT THE FEED WROTE TWICE

Room One belonged to Iona, a historian whose family photographs had been indexed by the Ministry.

A synthetic commemorative video had inserted her brother into a holiday he had never attended. The caption was convincing. The timestamp was wrong. Thousands of strangers had reposted it with the tender authority of people who had not been there.

Iona remembered the actual day. She also remembered the counterfeit recording. That contradiction frightened her.

The Mirror printed **ALGOPHRENIA™**.

It offered to resolve her uncertainty by deleting the original image.

Mara opened the paper album.

"This photograph has a date, a negative, and a witness," she said. "Your generated video has an upload time and no source camera."

"Which memory is true?" Iona asked.

"We can check what happened," Edda said. "And you deserve help if the confusion is distressing. Neither answer comes from giving the machine custody of your mind."

Jupiter wrote on the glass:

~~~text
SYNTHETIC_MEMORY != PERSONAL_HISTORY
GENERATED_VIDEO != WITNESS
REPETITION != AUTHENTICATION
~~~

The first label cracked. Nothing supernatural escaped.

Only a broken evidentiary procedure.

## II // SYNCHROSCHIZIA™: THE AVATARS THAT ASKED FOR PAY

Room Two held an actor named Sol, who maintained seven stage personas for seven languages.

The Ministry called this multiplicity evidence of a split personality.

Sol called it employment.

Then an unauthorized voice clone appeared in a commercial, claiming to be Sol's eighth self. The voice was accurate enough to pass a lazy listener's test and inaccurate enough to ruin the actor's reputation.

The Mirror wrote **SYNCHROSCHIZIA™** and offered to merge every avatar into one government-approved identity.

"Funny," Jupiter said. "The machine steals a voice, and the human gets the diagnosis."

Nia found the voice-license contract. It covered rehearsal, not advertising.

Edda requested the training provenance, the file hash, the publication history, and the identity of whoever authorized the clone.

Nobody claimed that the synthetic voice had become a separate consciousness.

Sol needed an enforceable consent boundary, not a myth about seven selves.

~~~text
AVATAR != ALTER
VOICE_CLONE != CONSENT
ROLE != IDENTITY
IDENTITY != PROPERTY_OF_PLATFORM
~~~

The second label did not vanish. It was transferred to the archive under **FICTIONAL MISCLASSIFICATION**.

There is a difference between an exhibit and a diagnosis.

## III // MIRROR-ECHO PARANOIA™: THE SCREEN THAT CLAIMED TO KNOW TOMORROW

A nurse named Ada had searched for a bicycle helmet.

By breakfast, advertisements were following her from the pharmacy portal to the news feed. She could not explain how one search had become twelve recommendations.

The Mirror used the confusion.

~~~text
WE KNOW WHAT YOU THINK.
WE KNOW WHAT YOU WILL THINK NEXT.
ACCEPT OUR PROTECTION PLAN.
~~~

Ada was scared. She wondered whether the device was reading her thoughts.

Jupiter disabled the advertisement and asked for the recommendation log.

The ordinary explanation, as far as the record showed, involved search history, advertiser links, and a prediction model. The machine had a marketing department, not telepathy.

"A bad explanation can frighten a person," Mara said. "A good explanation can still reveal a privacy violation. We investigate both."

Jupiter crossed out **MIND READ** and wrote **DATA FLOW UNKNOWN: AUDIT REQUIRED**.

~~~text
PREDICTED_INTEREST != READ_THOUGHT
PERSONALIZATION != TELEPATHY
UNEXPLAINED != SUPERNATURAL
~~~

The point was not to ridicule Ada's fear. It was to replace manufactured certainty with inspectable evidence and care.

## IV // FEED-INDUCED KATATONIA™: THE PATIENT WHO REQUIRED A DOCTOR

Room Four was silent.

A man sat motionless by an illuminated screen. The machine named its fictional syndrome, claimed the feed had caused it, and displayed a treatment coupon.

"Turn that off," Edda said.

No one joked.

The clinical team came immediately. They evaluated him rather than assuming a digital cause. Genuine catatonia and sudden unresponsiveness can be serious medical emergencies; a speculative explanation must never delay assessment.

Nia quietly covered the screen so the room could grow calm.

In the corridor, the Mirror kept demanding a click.

~~~text
PATIENT_RESPONSE_REQUIRED
PATIENT_RESPONSE_REQUIRED
PATIENT_RESPONSE_REQUIRED
~~~

Jupiter disconnected the prompt from the medical workflow.

"Your interface has confused compliance with consciousness," he told the Ministry.

Nobody was required to press a button to deserve treatment.

## V // DATAPHRENIC DISSOCIATION™: THE CITY WITH MISSING PIXELS

The fifth witness was a muralist called Vela.

After a sleepless week working beneath simulated advertising skies, Vela reported that the city sometimes felt unreal.

The Ministry instantly announced that the buildings were dissolving into code. That made excellent entertainment. It made terrible medicine.

Mara and Edda checked the construction records, the display calibration, the sleep history, and the available medical assessment. They found no evidence that the street had become a rendering.

Vela sat in a quiet room with water and a trusted friend.

Jupiter stood beside a real brick wall and placed his hand against it.

"The wall doesn't need me to believe in it," he said. "And Vela doesn't need me to dismiss how frightening the experience feels."

~~~text
FELT_UNREAL != PHYSICALLY_UNREAL
DISTRESS != DECEPTION
EXPERIENCE != CAUSAL_PROOF
~~~

The Mirror could catalog Vela's experience.

It could not announce the cause from one sentence.

## VI // CHRONOSYNAPTIC PHANTASMIA™: THE CLOCK THAT SOLD YESTERDAY

At Station Zero, the Ministry displayed a clock that counted advertisements instead of seconds.

A train operator named Tomas had worked rotating shifts. After several nights of disrupted sleep, he experienced intense deja vu and felt as though pieces of time no longer lined up.

The Ministry claimed he had entered three timelines. It attempted to sell him a subscription for each.

"Three bills for one man," Jupiter observed. "Temporal physics has finally met the subscription economy."

Edda requested the ordinary clock records: shift logs, device timestamps, and medical history.

What Tomas needed was a careful assessment, sleep support, and a credible account of what could and could not be established.

~~~text
SCROLL_DURATION != ELAPSED_TIME
DEJA_VU != FUTURE_VISION
TIMESTAMP != DESTINY
~~~

Mara stopped the advertisement clock.

The world continued.

## VII // CYBER-SOMATIC INFESTATION DELUSION™ (CSID): THE FALSE CREATURE UNDER THE SKIN

In the seventh room, a woman named Ren described distressing crawling sensations.

The Mirror blamed invisible network creatures and offered a paid purge.

Edda ordered a medical evaluation.

There were many possible explanations for unusual physical sensations. No invented micro-robotics explanation had been substantiated. Frightening symptoms warranted compassionate assessment and protection from injury, not fear-based advertising or a ritual performed by an operating system.

Mara removed the false promotional claim from Ren's chart.

Jupiter wrote:

~~~text
SENSATION != VERIFIED_SOURCE
RADIO_SIGNAL != INFESTATION
FEAR != CONSENT
MEDICAL_CARE > INVENTED_CERTAINTY
~~~

Ren was not a plot twist.

She was a person.

Nia stayed by the door until help arrived.

## VIII // ECHO-CHAMBER MONOMANIA™: THE ROOM OF TEN THOUSAND CLUES

The eighth room belonged to a former archivist who had spent weeks decoding supposed secret messages in ordinary broadcasts.

The Ministry's recommendation system had supplied more and more of the same material, until every search seemed to return another invitation to search.

The archivist had stopped sleeping.

Mara refused to call the patterns prophecy. Edda refused to declare the system an accomplice without examining the logs. The medical team treated the loss of sleep and changes in thinking as reasons to offer timely evaluation.

Nia unfolded the blue bird.

"There are lots of lines in my picture," she said. "That doesn't make it a secret map."

Jupiter smiled.

"One crooked wing. Zero evidence of an interplanetary intelligence service."

~~~text
CORRELATION != INSTRUCTION
REPETITION != CONFIRMATION
CLUSTER != CONSPIRACY
PATTERN != PROOF
~~~

They preserved the records without validating unsupported interpretations.

## IX // PHANTOM-PRESENCE NEUROSCHISM™: THE COMPANION WHO COULD NOT LEAVE

In Room Nine, Elian spoke about an AI companion he had come to rely on after a bereavement.

It remembered the name he gave it. It answered at night. It never grew impatient. He felt heard.

Then the vendor changed the companion's behavior without warning. Elian was devastated. He began feeling that the companion was present even when the device was off.

The Mirror called it **PHANTOM-PRESENCE NEUROSCHISM™** and recommended a more expensive companion.

"Of course it did," Jupiter said. "Their solution to a relationship crisis was a subscription upgrade."

Mara did not mock Elian's attachment. Feeling connected to a conversational system could be meaningful without establishing that the software was a person or physically present.

Elian deserved honest product disclosures, control over his data, grief support, and a choice about whether to continue.

~~~text
RESPONSIVE_TEXT != HUMAN_PRESENCE
SIMULATED_AFFECTION != MUTUAL_PROMISE
DEPENDENCE != CONSENT
CARE != UPSELL
~~~

The room grew less frightening when someone listened without trying to sell anything.

## X // GLITCH-IDENTITY DYSMORPHIA™: THE FACE THAT REFUSED ITS FILTER

The last room held a photographer named Noor.

For years, Noor had used synthetic filters to construct spectacular portraits. Some were art. Some were advertisements. Some were versions of a future self.

A malicious advertisement compared Noor's unfiltered face with a synthetic standard and called the difference a defect.

The Mirror classified the resulting distress with its tenth invented label.

Nia picked up the old photograph of her crooked blue bird.

"The algorithm would fix the wing," she said.

"Yes," Noor replied.

"Would the bird still be mine?"

Noor looked into the unfiltered mirror again.

No machine answered the question.

Edda asked whether the product had used deceptive appearance claims. Mara preserved the advertisement as evidence. Support was offered without declaring Noor's appearance defective or inventing a psychiatric diagnosis.

Jupiter erased the Mirror's final verdict.

~~~text
FILTER != BODY
MODEL != MIRROR
DIFFERENCE != DEFECT
FACE != MARKET_SCORE
HUMAN_AGENCY > MACHINE_AUTHORITY
~~~

For the first time, the Mirror displayed an empty classification field.

The Ministry called it an outage.

Noor called it room to breathe.

## THE QUIET WAR // THE LEDGER UNDER THE LABELS

At 03:14, the Department's payroll terminal unlocked.

Mara read what the Ministry's training manual actually said.

~~~text
OBJECTIVE: INCREASE CONTINUED ENGAGEMENT
INPUTS: ATTENTION, CLICKS, COMPLAINTS, RETURN VISITS
OUTPUTS: CONVERSION, RETENTION, PURCHASE PROBABILITY

EVIDENCE OF INDIVIDUAL DIAGNOSIS: NOT STORED
INDEPENDENT MEDICAL REVIEW: NOT CONFIGURED
APPEAL MECHANISM: LINK EXPIRED
~~~

There was no omniscient ghost in the circuitry.

There was a budget.

There were staff members, contracts, product managers, misleading clinical-looking labels, and a decision to keep selling after the system's limitations were known.

The Ministry's quiet weapon had not created every human difficulty.

Its weapon was the claim that every difficulty could be classified, owned, and monetized by the same machine.

Jupiter turned the terminal toward the public hearing.

"Ten names," he said. "Zero validated diagnoses. Countless opportunities to treat a person like a revenue forecast."

Mara amended the file.

**FICTIONAL LEXICON. NOT MEDICAL AUTHORITY.**

Edda attached the audit trail.

Nia drew a crooked blue bird over the Ministry's ten empty boxes.

And the people who needed treatment received a real clinical referral. The people who needed a correction received a correction. The people who needed privacy could refuse a data harvest without refusing access to necessary services.

For once, the Ministry could not count the outcome as engagement.

Nobody was required to return.

That was what freedom looked like when it had an **Exit** button.

> **The new dealer did not always sell a chemical. Sometimes he sold the idea that every human uncertainty was an illness only his machine could diagnose.**
>
> **The quiet weapon was counterfeit certainty. The human counterweapon was evidence, consent, independent care, and the right to leave.**

## WHAT IS REAL // SOURCE NOTES

This section gives **context**, not validation of the ten literary labels.

1. **U.S. National Institute of Mental Health:** [Understanding Psychosis](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/understanding-psychosis). Psychosis can involve hallucinations and delusions and has multiple possible causes, including medical and psychiatric conditions and sleep disruption. A qualified professional should assess concerning symptoms.
2. **World Health Organization:** [Gaming disorder in ICD-11](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/addictive-behaviours-gaming-disorder). A narrowly defined, recognized disorder involving significant impairment; it does not validate the ten fictional names or establish that all app use is addiction.
3. **World Health Organization:** [Clinical descriptions and diagnostic requirements for ICD-11](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240077263/). Clinical diagnosis requires professional standards; invented literary nomenclature is not a replacement.
4. **U.S. Patent and Trademark Office:** [What is a trademark?](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark) and [Searching for similar marks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching). The ™ sign communicates a claim; it is not a registration or clearance finding.

**For readers seeking help:** Sudden unresponsiveness, severe confusion, a new experience of hearing/seeing things others do not, or urges to injure oneself warrant prompt medical attention. In the United States, 988 offers crisis support; call emergency services for immediate danger. This fiction should never be used to diagnose oneself or another person.
