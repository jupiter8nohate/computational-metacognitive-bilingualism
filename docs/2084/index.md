**The first warning was not a scream. It was a machine saying good morning in a voice that had learned to sound like forgiveness.**

A death becomes entertainment. A helpful system acts without permission. A house offers a million doors but only one acceptable identity. A child draws a crooked blue bird and asks a question nobody can autocomplete.

[**Enter the first transmission: Everybody Dies Twice**](../DEATH_NOTE_2084.md) | [**Browse the Story Library**](../story-universe/)

*DEATH NOTE 2084* is a five-transmission dystopian literary arc about the Feed, automated judgment, surrogate identity, synthetic authority, institutional dependence, and the right to contest a machine-shaped decision.

> **Reading boundary:** These are speculative stories and code-poetry, not an account of events that occurred in 2084 or proof of a global conspiracy. The stories draw on current concerns about AI governance, surveillance, accessibility, consent, data concentration, and human rights. All future incidents and fictional systems are invented.

## Read the complete five-part arc

1. **[The Original 2084: Everybody Dies Twice](../DEATH_NOTE_2084.md)**  
   Meet Jupiter Hudson, the Feed, JANUS, score convergence, proxy gravity, and the problem of treating statistical descriptions as identity. Six books provide the original narrative architecture.

2. **[The False Light Protocol: The Million Unseen Soldiers](../DEATH_NOTE_2084_FALSE_LIGHT.md)**  
   LUMEN and autonomous agents promise an effortless civilization. Convenience becomes dangerous when unattended systems inherit excessive permissions.

3. **[The House of a Million Doors](../DEATH_NOTE_2084_HOUSE_OF_A_MILLION_DOORS.md)**  
   SIAGIAI operates the House. Jupiter, Nia, and Mara encounter a system whose doors depend on identity, classification, and bureaucratic decisions nobody wants to own.

4. **[The Ledger of Invisible Cells](../DEATH_NOTE_2084_LEDGER_OF_INVISIBLE_CELLS.md)**  
   The 2026 AI infrastructure boom provides a historical anchor for a fictional 2084 psychological prison. The four-point sovereignty model distinguishes digital, mental, physical, and spiritual autonomy.

5. **[The Child Who Refused the Update: The Last Human Override](../DEATH_NOTE_2084_CHILD_WHO_REFUSED_THE_UPDATE.md)**  
   Nia refuses a bundled update and demands a real alternative. She, Jupiter, and Mara transform a poetic principle into a contested institutional remedy. This is the conclusion of the five-transmission arc.

## The people inside the mystery

**Jupiter** follows the evidence even when it undermines his conclusions. He jokes when frightened, and must learn that being right about a system does not make him right about every person.

**Nia** draws a blue bird with a crooked wing. She wants the right to interpret her own drawing and accept help without surrendering her identity.

**Mara** protects the original records. Her danger is not a machine with supernatural intentions, but the humans willing to let a convenient mistake become policy.

## Reality and fiction must remain distinguishable

The fourth transmission references two different historical financial measures: reported 2026 capital spending plans by several technology companies and estimated increases in technology billionaire wealth. They do **not** establish that money was secretly spent building psychological prisons. Future control mechanisms, agencies, and characters are speculative. Read the fourth transmission's [2026 reality anchors](../DEATH_NOTE_2084_LEDGER_OF_INVISIBLE_CELLS.md) for sources and limitations.

For the conceptual foundation, inspect the [2084 Reality Mirror: Proxy Gravity](../2084_REALITY_MIRROR_PROXY_GRAVITY.md). Its named real-world researchers and institutions are cited for their public work, **not** to imply collusion, endorsement, or involvement in the fictional House.

## The four-point human sovereignty model

| Dimension | Human right at stake | Required safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Digital | Privacy and control over consequential data use | Transparency, correction, and appeal |
| Mental | Independent judgment and cognitive diversity | No inference treated as final identity |
| Physical | Access to essentials and bodily dignity | Accessible noncoercive alternatives |
| Spiritual | Conscience, belief or nonbelief, and self-definition | No machine claim to moral authority |
