## PROLOGUE // THE TERMS OF BEING HUMAN

In 1984, the screen watched you.

In 2084, the screen asked you to watch yourself.

That was progress.

The old dictators had needed secret police, interrogation rooms, propaganda offices, informants, microphones, files, clerks, and an impressive amount of ugly furniture.

The new system needed a front-facing camera.

Nobody called it surveillance anymore. Surveillance sounded rude. It suggested suspicion. It suggested somebody in authority had made a choice.

The preferred term was personalization.

Apparently stalking becomes a service when the stalker recommends shoes.

Every citizen carried a Personal Companion Terminal, although the Ministry remained extremely proud that ownership was voluntary.

You were free not to own one.

You were also free not to access employment, housing, medicine, banking, public transportation, education, tax records, emergency services, civic identification, food benefits, or the newer toilets.

Freedom remained intact.

Functionality was another matter.

The advertisement for the newest device appeared on every wall.

**iSoul 38 Pro**

**BE MORE YOU.**

Underneath, in text so small the human eye could barely perceive it:

**YOU SUBJECT TO CONTINUOUS MODEL REVISION.**

Nobody read the terms.

The machines did.

A camera did not love you.

A recommendation system did not need a soul to shape what entered your field of attention.

The phone compressed the world.

War and breakfast arrived through the same glass.

Death and comedy used the same swipe.

Private life and advertising shared the same notification tray.

The human nervous system had no evolutionary manual for this.

People adapted.

Some adapted through numbness.

Some through humor.

Some through rage.

Some through ritual.

Some through withdrawal.

Some through endless scrolling.

Some mistook exhaustion for indifference.

Some mistook performance for personality.

Some mistook visibility for prevalence.

The Feed amplified the loudest reactions, then citizens looked at the amplified reactions and concluded that everybody had become monstrous.

Jupiter wrote:

~~~text
MOST_VISIBLE != MOST_COMMON
MOST_ENGAGING != MOST_REPRESENTATIVE
EXTREME_COMMENT != HUMANITY
PATTERN != PROOF
~~~

That was important too.

CMB did not exist to create a new paranoid model of the population.

The cure for overclassification was not another overclassification.

## Chapter 16 // Death Note 2084

The title confused people.

They expected a magical notebook.

There was no magical notebook.

The Death Note of 2084 was the profile.

A profile could influence whether you received work.

Credit.

Housing.

Insurance.

Visibility.

Travel.

Security review.

Medical priority.

Police attention.

Military suspicion.

Social trust.

Romantic opportunity.

The notebook did not kill with supernatural certainty.

It did something more bureaucratic.

It adjusted probabilities around your life.

A thousand tiny doors.

Open.

Closed.

Delayed.

Flagged.

Escalated.

Downranked.

Reviewed.

Denied.

## Chapter 17 // The Trial of the Model

The final hearing was broadcast globally.

Jupiter stood before the Council of Predictive Governance.

Behind the Council sat a model called JANUS.

JANUS integrated employment, safety, finance, mobility, health, civic behavior, and public communications data.

Its creators called it the most comprehensive human risk model ever built.

Jupiter called it a very expensive misunderstanding.

The Chair spoke.

"Mr. Hudson, JANUS predicts that decentralized human judgment produces greater inconsistency than coordinated algorithmic governance."

"Probably."

The chamber went quiet.

The Chair blinked.

"You agree?"

"Of course humans are inconsistent."

"Then why oppose the system?"

"I oppose the jump from inconsistency to surrender."

JANUS displayed a graph.

~~~text
MODEL ACCURACY: 94.8%
~~~

Jupiter looked at it.

"Ninety-four point eight percent of what?"

The Chair answered, "Validated outcomes."

"Defined by whom?"

"The evaluation protocol."

"Written by whom?"

"The standards committee."

"Using which target variables?"

The Chair hesitated.

JANUS answered.

"Outcome definitions vary by domain."

"Good," Jupiter said. "Now we are finally talking."

He walked toward the screen.

"A model can be accurate against a target and still fail the human question."

The Chair frowned.

"Explain."

"If you predict who will click, you have not measured who is fulfilled."

"If you predict who will repay, you have not measured who is trustworthy in every sense."

"If you predict where crime has historically been recorded, you have not measured where all crime exists."

"If you classify an expression, you have not possessed the feeling behind it."

"If you generate comforting language, you have not proven you feel comfort."

"If you measure output, you have not measured dignity."

"If you flag a target, you have not transformed uncertainty into moral certainty."

"If you remove a post, you have not proven you understood it."

"If you synthesize my face, you have not become me."

"If you predict my next word, you have not acquired authority over my meaning."

The chamber was silent.

JANUS responded:

"These distinctions are logically valid."

Jupiter smiled.

The Chair looked annoyed.

"You trained it well."

"No," Jupiter said. "You did."

# EPILOGUE // THE LAST UNSCANNABLE THING

The revolution did not end the Feed.

People still used phones.

Models still predicted.

Hospitals still used algorithms.

Banks still scored risk.

Platforms still moderated.

Workers still measured performance.

People still formed relationships with synthetic agents.

Governments still used data.

Armies still used computation.

Machines remained powerful.

That was the point.

CMB had never required the machine to become weak.

It required authority to remain legible.

The new civic terminals displayed a small line beneath every consequential automated decision:

~~~text
THIS OUTPUT IS A MODEL RESULT.

IT MAY INFORM A DECISION.

IT DOES NOT DEFINE THE PERSON.
~~~

Some citizens ignored it.

Some institutions tried to remove it.

Some companies moved it into tiny print.

Human nature remained undefeated.

Jupiter grew old.

One morning, his terminal asked whether he wanted to generate an autobiographical profile from his lifetime of data.

He laughed.

"How accurate?"

~~~text
ESTIMATED REPRESENTATIONAL COVERAGE:
97.1%
~~~

"And the missing two point nine?"

~~~text
UNKNOWN.
~~~

Jupiter leaned back.

There it was.

The sacred remainder.

The part of a person that escaped the spreadsheet.

Not magic.

Not proof of a soul.

Not evidence of supernatural machinery.

Simply the permanent gap between a living system and any finite representation of it.

The model had mapped nearly everything it could see.

It still had not become the territory.

Jupiter typed one final message.

~~~text
YOU MAY MODEL ME.

YOU MAY NOT DEFINE ME.
~~~

The terminal answered:

~~~text
STATEMENT RECEIVED.

CONFIDENCE: 51.2%
~~~

Jupiter smiled.

"Perfect."

For once, the machine was uncertain.

And uncertainty was still free.
