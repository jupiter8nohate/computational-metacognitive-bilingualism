# Cognitive Sovereignty

**Canonical definition.** In CMB, cognitive sovereignty is the principle that people retain authority over their own meaning, self-definition, attention, consent, and cognitive identity even when computational systems model or predict them.

## Formal expression

```text
SELF_DEFINITION > EXTERNAL_PROFILE
CONSENT > INFERRED_PERMISSION
```

## Why it matters

Modern systems can build increasingly detailed representations of people. CMB uses cognitive sovereignty to keep those representations from silently becoming ownership of identity, intention, or meaning.

## Example

A platform can personalize a feed while still allowing the user to inspect, correct, reject, or limit inferred attributes rather than treating the inferred profile as final truth.

## Interpretation boundaries

Cognitive sovereignty is an authored CMB framework term and policy concept, not a claim that every jurisdiction already recognizes an identically named legal right.
