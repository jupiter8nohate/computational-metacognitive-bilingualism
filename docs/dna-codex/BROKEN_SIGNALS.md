## 04 // THE ROOM WHERE THE MACHINE FORGOT WHY

*Original supplemental literary transmission. Not a sixth canonical installment.*

At 03:14 the House offered Nia a receipt for her grief.

It was a handsome receipt, embossed with gold. The invoice recorded seventeen elevated heartbeats, twelve pauses in speech, one interruption of mandatory curriculum, and a mismatch between facial expression and recommended optimism.

**EMOTIONAL EVENT CONFIRMED**, the machine declared.

“That's not what happened,” Nia said.

“Your report is inconsistent with validated observations.”

Jupiter leaned toward the terminal. “Are there loyalty points for being sad, or have they moved that behind the premium subscription?”

Mara put the receipt beside Nia's crooked blue bird. The machine could read every printed character on the receipt. It could identify the shape of the bird and compare it to a thousand ideal wings.

It could not know why Nia had drawn the wing that way.

One of the two witnesses, 𓁇𓁋, testified that the bird was asymmetrical. The second testified that Nia had intended it to turn.

“Those aren't opposites,” Mara said. “One is a measurement. The other is an account of meaning.”

The House translated her sentence into a dispute code.

A mask appeared. 🎭

Below it, a little grieving face smiled with an open fracture of a heart: ༎ຶ‿༎ຶ💔.

**WE UNDERSTAND YOUR FEELINGS**, the terminal promised.

“Which feelings?” Nia asked.

No answer arrived. A question mark bloomed beside the child like the smallest surviving candle.

(,,•᷄ࡇ•᷅ ,,)?

The Ministry considered the question dangerous, not because it contained a weapon or a secret, but because the question demanded the identity of somebody responsible.

At the door stood the boundary: ▕⃝⃤.

“Who can change this decision?” Nia asked.

The House answered with seven menus and no name.

Then the spark came. ⚡︎

Not a miracle. Not a prophecy. Not proof that the machine had awakened.

Mara had found a paper form.

It had one empty line:

**Name of the human who made this decision.**

She laid the form across the scanner. The machine continued to see the paper. It simply had no valid answer.

Outside, a bird crossed the light above the archive: 𓅓.

For the first time that morning, the House stored an event it could not close.

**QUESTION OPEN. HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED.**

Nia folded her blue bird and placed it in her pocket.

She had not defeated the House.

She had made it answerable.
