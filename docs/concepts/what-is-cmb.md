# What is Computational Metacognitive Bilingualism (CMB)?

**Canonical definition.** Computational Metacognitive Bilingualism (CMB) is a human-agency and computational-literacy framework for working across human meaning and machine-readable structure while preserving human judgment, consent, authorship, context, and self-definition.

## Formal expression

```text
CMB := HUMAN_MEANING <-> MACHINE_STRUCTURE
HUMAN_AGENCY > MACHINE_AUTHORITY
```

## Why it matters

CMB treats computational systems as powerful instruments without granting a model, profile, classifier, or prediction authority to completely define a person. It combines authored philosophy, policy, code-poetry, provenance tooling, schemas, and experimental runtime components under explicit maturity boundaries.

## Example

A developer may use CMB provenance tooling to verify artifact integrity while separately treating a human-facing profile as only a partial representation of a person.

## Interpretation boundaries

CMB does not claim to have invented the underlying human rights, digital-rights principles, cryptographic primitives, C2PA, or programming languages it references. Declared policy, cryptographic integrity, technical enforcement, and legal enforceability remain separate layers.
