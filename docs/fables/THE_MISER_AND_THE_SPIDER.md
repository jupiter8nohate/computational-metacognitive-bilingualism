## Prologue // The Smallest Debt

Jupiter did not notice the web until the fly stopped moving.

It hung between two windowpanes in a forgotten chapel. Outside, the marketplace had closed. Inside, a priest counted coins beside a ledger. The man had written every debt neatly, as if neat handwriting might absolve the numbers.

A widow owed six.

A laborer owed twelve.

A family owed more than it earned.

The priest closed the book when Jupiter approached.

"Are you collecting for charity?" Jupiter asked.

"For order," the priest answered.

From the window came a vibration too slight to hear. A spider crossed its thread without touching the stones of the building that sheltered it.

Jupiter understood the difference between a chapel and a web.

One offered refuge.

The other could make a living from the arrival of the helpless.

He looked again at the ledger.

"Who is permitted to leave?" he asked.

## I // The Medieval Witness

Some medieval Christian writers used the spider as a moral image of greed, temptation, or exploitation. Their accounts are **literary and theological readings**, not universal beliefs about spiders.

The early thirteenth-century preacher **Odo of Cheriton** made that metaphor unusually precise. In one fable, a spider seizes a small fly but hides when a louder wasp or bee arrives. Odo compares this behavior to certain powerful church officials who pursue poor people for offenses or accusations, but retreat when threatened by the wealthy.

The target of that story is not the insect. It is **selective accountability**.

In another fable, a spider's labor to catch one fly becomes a criticism of people who exhaust themselves seeking ecclesiastical wealth. Elsewhere, a wasp enters a web and discovers too late that the attractive net is a trap, an allegory of destructive temptation.

These episodes are discussed in Megan Cavell's peer-reviewed study of Odo's fables and medieval bestiaries. [Read the scholarly source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11061-020-09645-7).

The lesson sharpens when the web is treated as an institution instead of an insect.

A poor person meets punishment.

A powerful person meets an exception.

The same law has become two different doors.

## II // The Prophet's Garment

Isaiah 59:5-8 uses eggs, webs, and crooked paths in a condemnation of wrongdoing. Its webs cannot serve as garments or cover their makers' deeds.

It is not a prediction of the World Wide Web. But it offers a striking contemporary metaphor: **the infrastructure of a lie cannot become protection simply because the lie has been carefully woven**.

And 1 Peter 5:8 calls the Devil a *roaring lion*, not a spider. Comparing the Devil's temptations to a spiderweb belongs to later Christian allegorical interpretation, not the literal wording of Peter's image.

Jupiter stood between the ledger and the window.

"If the robe is made of silk," he asked the priest, "does it matter where the silk came from?"

The man answered without looking up.

"It matters who is allowed to examine the loom."

## III // The New Miser

Centuries later, the counting room had changed.

The candles had become screens. The ledger had become a database. The marketplace had learned to remain open all night.

Jupiter entered the Net to retrieve a memory. A recommendation interrupted him. Then another. A frightened headline led to an argument; the argument led to a video; the video offered a reason to stay.

The machine asked for no coins.

Instead, his pauses, clicks, and attention became measurable signals. Some online services use those signals to personalize recommendations or advertising; such systems can be useful, but commercial incentives can also encourage excessive engagement.

"I paid nothing," Jupiter said.

"That depends," replied the Spider, "on what you believe your time is worth."

He remembered the widow in the old chapel. Their situations were not equivalent: financial exploitation and online attention design are distinct harms, with different causes and remedies.

But both raised a common question.

**Who receives the benefit, who carries the cost, and who can refuse the bargain?**

## IV // The Three Ledgers

| Ledger | What the keeper measures | What the keeper may overlook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The medieval miser** | Debts, payment, and vulnerability | The dignity and circumstances of the debtor |
| **The unequal authority** | Alleged offenses and status | Equal treatment and due process |
| **The digital attention market** | Engagement, conversion, and retention | The person's time, context, and well-being |

Not every market, medieval cleric, or digital platform is exploitative. The analogy concerns **mechanisms of extraction and unequal power**, not a claim that the entire internet is malicious.

Jupiter opened his notebook and wrote a record no merchant could alter by changing a number on a screen:

```text
♃ D.N.A. // THE MISER'S LEDGER

VALUE != PRICE
METRIC != MEANING
PATTERN != PROOF
PROFILE != PERSON
ENGAGEMENT != LOVE
ATTENTION != CONSENT
POWER_TO_CLASSIFY != RIGHT_TO_DEFINE

HUMAN_AGENCY > MACHINE_AUTHORITY
```

## V // A Web That Cannot Cover

The Spider returned to the window.

"You cannot stop everyone from building nets," it said.

"No," Jupiter replied. "Nor should I. Webs can connect people. Their value depends on their design, their uses, and the freedom left to the people who encounter them."

The priest's ledger was gone. In its place sat a blank sheet of paper.

Jupiter wrote three questions upon it.

**Can a person see what is being taken?**

**Can a person challenge the judgment being made?**

**Can a person leave without losing everything that matters?**

The Spider offered no answer.

That was not proof it had been defeated.

It was the beginning of an audit.

The miser's oldest trick had been to call extraction a service. Jupiter would not mistake either word for evidence.

When he left, the widow's name was still in the ledger.

He did not declare her free because he had understood her story.

He went back to find her.

> **The spider does not need to own the world. It needs only a system in which the smallest creature cannot challenge the keeper of the web.**
